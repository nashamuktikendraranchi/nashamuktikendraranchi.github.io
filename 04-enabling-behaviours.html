Enabling Behaviours Families Often Miss Around Addiction

Enabling is a blunt word, and many families bristle when they first hear it. It does not mean you caused the addiction, and it does not mean you are a fool. It means that, in trying to keep the peace or protect the family's name, you may have removed the very consequences that might have pushed the person to accept help. In homes around Ranchi, this often looks like love under pressure, not like a theory from a book.

What enabling often looks like in practice

You repay a loan the person took in someone else's name. You call a supervisor with a story about fever. You pour leftover liquor down the drain, then buy the peace offering they demand in the evening. You let them sleep through the day and wake the rest of the household at night. You hide the problem from elders, or you hide the elders' concern from the person. You hand over cash "just for travel" when you already know the route goes past a shop.

None of these acts makes you a bad relative. They make the addiction easier to continue. The person does not have to face a missed shift, an empty wallet, or a family that will no longer pretend. Shame then stays inside the house, and the drinking stays too.

Why stopping these habits feels cruel

If you suddenly refuse money or refuse to lie, there may be a storm. The person may say you have abandoned them, or that a good parent would not let them go hungry. Neighbours may hear the argument. This is usually the hardest week, not because the new limit is wrong, but because the old pattern is being interrupted. It helps to decide the limits in a calm hour, not in the middle of a fight, and to tell one trusted person outside the immediate quarrel so that you are not alone when the pressure returns.

Changing enabling is not the same as throwing someone out with nowhere to go, and it is not the same as humiliating them in front of a crowd. What you can stop offering is the cover story, the secret cash, and the endless resetting of last night as if it did not happen.

A clearer kind of help

Sit down, if it is safe to do so, and name two or three things that will change. For example: no cash in hand this month; no calls to the employer with false reasons; a medical assessment this week. Then keep the promise. If you cannot keep it, say so honestly and choose a smaller limit you can actually hold.
